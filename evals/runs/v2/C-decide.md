My recommendation is to **expense it as repairs and maintenance**. The final call is yours, though, because a few facts only you can check could change the answer.

**Why expense:**
- The slates were replaced like-for-like after storm damage. That puts the roof back to its earlier condition without improving it, which makes it a repair. Under IAS 16 and FRS 102 Section 17, that kind of cost is expensed.
- You lease the warehouse, so the building isn't your asset. You'd only capitalise if the work were a leasehold improvement that adds something new, and like-for-like replacement doesn't.
- UK tax treatment points the same way. Repairing part of a building, not replacing the whole asset, is normally a deductible revenue expense.

**What would change the answer:**
1. **Improvement:** if the new slates or underlay are clearly better than the old ones (say, a new insulated system rather than just slates), part of the cost may need to be capitalised.
2. **Replacing the whole roof:** if you capitalised the roof separately as a component, a full replacement might be capitalised, with the old roof written off. Patching damaged slates isn't that.
3. **The lease terms:** check who is responsible for structural repairs. If it's the landlord, the cost may be recoverable from them. If you already hold a dilapidations provision, the cost may use that up rather than going through profit.
4. **Insurance:** if you're claiming on storm cover, record the expected payout separately (as a receivable once it's virtually certain). Don't reduce the £18,000 by it.
